NexGene

A health-tech prototype exploring predictive approaches to disease prevention using genomic, biomarker, molecular, and lifestyle data.

Role Founder · Product Design · Concept
Type Health-Tech Prototype
Status Ongoing
Focus Clarity · Prevention · Data UX

The problem

Most digital health experiences are reactive. By the time a person opens an app or receives a notification, something has usually already gone wrong or become urgent. The dominant model is still “wait for symptoms or a lab result, then respond.”

At the same time, the volume of personal health data is exploding — genomic sequencing is becoming cheaper, wearable and lifestyle signals are continuous, and molecular and biomarker insights are increasingly accessible. The opportunity is not more data. It is turning multi-layered signals into a coherent, understandable, and trustworthy product experience focused on prevention and precision.

What NexGene explores

NexGene is a product concept and ongoing prototype that asks: what would a prevention-oriented health experience look like if it treated genomic, biomarker, molecular, and lifestyle data as parts of one picture rather than isolated reports?

The work sits at the intersection of product design, information architecture, and the clinical reality that early signals are probabilistic, incomplete, and easy to misinterpret. The design challenge is to make complexity legible without creating false certainty.

Core design challenges

  • Legibility of dense data — Genomic and molecular information is inherently complex. The interface must support progressive disclosure so users can go deeper without being overwhelmed on first contact.
  • Trust without overclaiming — In health, visual confidence can be dangerous. The product language must communicate uncertainty, limitations, and the difference between insight and diagnosis.
  • Multi-source coherence — Different data types (genomic risk scores, biomarkers, lifestyle patterns) arrive at different frequencies and with different reliability. The experience needs a consistent hierarchy and status language.
  • Emotional tone — The visual and interaction system must feel intelligent, calm, and serious rather than gamified or alarmist.

Approach so far

The current phase has focused on product framing, information architecture, and early interface directions rather than high-fidelity visual polish. Key decisions include:

  • Treating prevention as the primary orientation instead of post-diagnosis management
  • Designing for progressive disclosure so the first surface stays scannable while deeper layers remain available
  • Building a restrained visual system (dark, minimal, editorial) that supports focus and reduces noise
  • Keeping the prototype explicitly non-clinical — this is an exploration of product experience, not a diagnostic tool

Because I am also a medical student, the work is informed by clinical reasoning: what information is actionable, what is still research-grade, and how to avoid design that implies certainty the underlying data does not support.

Current status

NexGene remains an active prototype. The emphasis is on clarifying the product concept, testing information hierarchy, and iterating on the core experience of multi-layered health signals. Deeper case material, user flows, and interface explorations will be added as the project develops.

Important note

NexGene is a conceptual and design exploration. It is not a medical device, diagnostic system, or clinical product. Nothing in this project should be interpreted as medical advice or a substitute for professional care.
