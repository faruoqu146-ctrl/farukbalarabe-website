Sable

Decision intelligence for founders and operators who treat judgment as a craft — not another notes app or AI chat.

Role Product Design · UX · System
Type Product Concept · Figma System
Focus Clarity · Restraint · Hierarchy
Deliverable Full UI kit · Screens · Prototype

The problem

Important decisions are made with incomplete context, poorly documented, rarely reviewed, and never systematically improved. Founders and operators lose the ability to learn from their own judgment because the tools they use are built for tasks, notes, or chat — not for the quality of decisions over time.

Sable was designed as a specialized instrument: capture full context, force clarity through structure (options, criteria, confidence), track outcomes, and surface patterns in judgment. The anti-productivity product — not about doing more, about deciding better.

Product hypothesis

If high-agency professionals had a quiet, precise place to record the full shape of a decision and later record what actually happened, the quality of their judgment would compound. The interface should feel like it understands priorities, reduce unnecessary decisions, and never shout for attention.

Design principles

  • Intelligence — Surface what matters. Reduce decisions about the interface itself.
  • Restraint — Every element has purpose. No decorative noise.
  • Precision — Exact spacing, alignment, and hierarchy.
  • Darkness — Deep neutrals and controlled contrast as visual intelligence, not a theme.
  • Confidence — Strong hierarchy without shouting.
  • Unconventional — Occasional editorial structure and unexpected but useful patterns.

What was designed

  • Full design system: color tokens, type scale, spacing, radius, elevation
  • Component library: buttons, inputs, search, cards, badges, tabs, toast, modal, avatar
  • Core screens: Login, Onboarding, Dashboard, Decision Detail, Search, Settings
  • States: Empty, Loading, Error, Success
  • Mobile adaptation of primary flows
  • Interactive prototype connections across the main journey
  • Marketing frame and documentation for commercial presentation

Visual language

Near-black surfaces, soft bone whites, muted oxidized-bronze accent used sparingly. Editorial typography, controlled density, thin accent rules as a signature detail. The product is meant to look expensive because it is considered — not because of effects.

Primary flows

  • Entry → Sign in → Onboarding (focus area) → Dashboard
  • Dashboard → Decision Detail (context · options · criteria · outcome)
  • Search across decisions and notes
  • Resolve → Success with outcome tracking reminder

Outcome

A commercially coherent product system that sits at the intersection of product thinking, UX precision, visual restraint, and distinctive identity. Suitable as a portfolio case, Figma Community / marketplace template foundation, or client presentation piece.
